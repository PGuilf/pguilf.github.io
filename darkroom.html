Building a floating darkroom with Fable 5

Open the darkroom

I've taken pictures on film for fun over the years, across a few different Tumblrs and Insta accounts. A grid of thumbnails was never a great way to show them.

The idea, in bed: I loved the darkroom back in my uni days. I had the idea of a developer tray you could look down into, prints submerged in the chemistry, drifting and bobbing on the surface. You reach in, pull one out, look at it. That's the whole thing. Simple on purpose, just a nicer way to show some images. I built out a rough version in Claude.

The day after: I dropped back into Claude for a while and worked on the parts that make it feel real — the safelight glow, the way the liquid surface catches and moves, the drift and bob of each print. A lot of trial and error. Much of it came together with Fable 5, which I got to use just before it was taken off. Claude Code drafted the build; I directed it, refined it, and decided when each part felt right.

Under the surface: One file, pure HTML, CSS and vanilla JS, no frameworks. Only the prints you can see exist at any moment, built and pruned as the camera drifts. Each gets its own phase, speed and tilt, so overlapping waves give an organic, non-repeating float. Layered gradients, SVG turbulence and animated caustics fake the red light and the moving liquid; the tray rim sits in front and moves against the mouse for depth. A single requestAnimationFrame loop drives it all, with the expensive layers dropping out while you drag.
