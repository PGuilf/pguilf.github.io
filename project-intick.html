Intick: A new approach to futures trading

I was the founding product designer at Intick, a fintech startup building a modern futures trading platform. I led design from 0 to 1 — building the product, design system, and research practice from scratch. The target users were professional traders working on institutional desks, people who spend their days staring at Bloomberg terminals and legacy OMS platforms.

UX UI Research 0 to 1 Design System Fintech

The challenge: replacing a legacy terminal

Futures traders are deeply embedded in legacy infrastructure: EMSX, Charles River, and proprietary OMS platforms built for institutional desks. These systems are data-dense, keyboard-driven, and optimised for speed over usability. Traders develop precise muscle memory around them: shortcut sequences, visual scanning patterns, order entry flows they can execute without thinking. The challenge wasn’t to simplify or prettify — it was to match that fluency in a modern interface. Breaking familiar mental models mid-trade isn’t a UX inconvenience; it’s a risk event. Every decision around information hierarchy, data density, and interaction speed was weighed against how a trader actually behaves under pressure.

Research

I established the research practice from the ground up. I conducted moderated user interviews with active traders, simulating multiple desk roles — buy-side trader, portfolio manager, sales trader, and execution broker. This gave us a rounded picture of the ecosystem and the different ways each persona would interact with the platform. I ran usability tests on the live build and on Figma prototypes across multiple iterations, tagging and synthesising findings in Airtable.

    Recurring themes from research

  • • Order book orientation not matching trader expectations
  • • Cold start state looked broken with no live data
  • • Structure of the order book unclear (bid/ask separation)
  • • Adding an order lacked open fields — felt unfamiliar
  • • Notifications needed a unique sound and better positioning
  • • Traders expected to trade directly from the parent order, not via the order book
  • • New parent card and status visibility tested strongly
  • • "Makes a lot more sense, I can really see what’s going on here"

Design exploration

I explored three structural approaches for the main trading view, evaluating how to surface active orders, the order book, and markets simultaneously on a single screen — a core requirement for traders who need everything at a glance. Structure 1 emerged as the strongest: parent cards on the left, order books scrolling horizontally, with working orders visible below. It performed best for responsiveness and cognitive load.

I then moved into high-fidelity prototypes across two concepts, testing both with traders in moderated sessions. Results were closely correlated — both felt familiar, with clear preference for the new parent card design and the responsive component system. I iterated on key components including the summary box (the trade confirmation panel), trading order cards, filters, permissions, notifications, and the RFQ flow. The summary box went through the most rounds — we tested five distinct scenarios covering single aggressive trades, passive trades, combinations, residuals, and error handling before landing on a final pattern that traders understood without guidance.
