The challenge: medication non-adherence at scale

Medication non-adherence is one of the most costly and preventable problems in healthcare. Patients miss doses, forget to reorder, or disengage from managing repeat prescriptions — leading to poorer health outcomes and avoidable strain on the NHS. The question wasn't just how to remind people, but when and how to do it in a way that actually changes behaviour. A generic push notification at the wrong moment is easy to ignore. The challenge was to make the intervention feel timely, relevant, and frictionless enough that patients would act on it.

Concept and research

I worked through the problem from concept, starting with research to understand how patients actually manage their prescriptions day-to-day. We spoke to a range of patients across different medication types — chronic conditions, regular repeat prescriptions, and more variable needs. What came through clearly was that the problem wasn't awareness, it was friction. People knew they needed to reorder. Life got in the way.

From there, I mapped the end-to-end journey — where patients were dropping off, what triggered action, and what a proactive intervention needed to feel like to be trusted rather than ignored. Healthcare communication has to be calm and precise. The language, timing, and hierarchy of information all carry more weight than in a typical consumer product.

Design and testing

The predictive model does the heavy lifting — identifying when a patient is due their next supply based on their individual dispensing patterns and proactively triggering the process before they run out. My job was to design the patient-facing experience around it: the reminder surface, the confirmation flow, and the edge case states — patients who've recently ordered, those on variable doses, those who've paused.

I iterated through multiple rounds of user testing, refining the interaction model and the copy at each stage. Small things mattered a lot here — the difference between a patient acting on a reminder and dismissing it often came down to the phrasing of a single line. We tested different tones, different entry points, and different confirmation patterns until we landed on something that felt trustworthy and effortless.
